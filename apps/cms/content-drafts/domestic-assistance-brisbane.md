## What domestic assistance may include

Domestic assistance generally means practical help with household activities. Depending on a person's needs and the service agreement, this may include routine cleaning, laundry, changing bed linen, washing dishes, basic meal preparation or keeping frequently used areas tidy. Agree on tasks and frequency in advance. A listing is a starting point, not confirmation that a provider offers every task or can meet a particular schedule.

It can help to describe the outcome that matters rather than relying only on a broad service label. For example, someone might need support to keep the kitchen usable, manage laundry on a regular day, or complete a short list of household tasks after another appointment. A clear description helps a provider explain whether the request fits their service, what they would include and whether they have capacity.

Domestic assistance is different from some forms of personal care, nursing or clinical treatment. If a task involves health care, medication, transfers, personal hygiene or another specialised activity, ask the provider who will perform it and what qualifications, registration or training apply. Do not assume that a domestic-assistance listing covers clinical or personal-care tasks.

## Choosing support that fits your routine

Start by listing the tasks that are most important and how often they need to happen. Note any preferences that affect the arrangement, such as a consistent day, a particular time window, communication needs, cultural considerations, pets in the home or products that should not be used. Share only information needed to discuss the service; private details can be discussed directly with a provider through an appropriate channel.

Consider how much assistance is useful. Some people want a worker to complete agreed tasks; others prefer to participate or direct how things are done. Explain your preferences and ask how the provider will follow them. If routines change, ask how to request changes and how much notice is needed.

## Questions to ask providers

Before choosing a domestic-assistance provider in Brisbane, ask for a written explanation of the proposed service. Useful questions include:

- Which household tasks are included, and which are not?
- Who will attend, and can the same worker visit regularly?
- What days and times are currently available?
- What happens if a worker is sick or a visit must be cancelled?
- Are cleaning products and equipment supplied, or should the household provide them?
- How are preferences, access instructions and changes recorded?
- What is the price, how is travel handled, and are there cancellation charges?
- What screening, insurance and complaint processes apply?

Ask the provider to explain unfamiliar terms. Request a written service agreement covering tasks, timing, charges, responsibilities and how to raise concerns. Compare the full arrangement, not only the headline price.

## Funding and costs

Funding depends on the person's circumstances, current plan or approval, and the service being arranged. Do not assume a household task is automatically covered. Check with the relevant coordinator or agency when needed, and confirm billing with the provider before support starts.

Ask for a breakdown of charges, including rates, minimum booking length, travel, supplies and cancellation terms. If a plan manager handles payment, confirm required documents and whether the service fits the person's arrangements. Keep copies of quotes, agreements and invoices.

Prices and funding rules can change. Treat directory information as a prompt for questions, not financial or eligibility advice, and confirm current details before committing to ongoing support.

## Safety, privacy and quality

Because support takes place at home, discuss keys, entry arrangements, alarms, items that should not be moved and areas that are out of scope. Agree how unexpected situations will be handled and whom to contact if the person cannot answer the door.

Ask how the provider screens workers, protects personal information and handles complaints or incidents. Confirm whom to contact about incomplete tasks or concerns. A directory listing does not guarantee an outcome; verify relevant credentials and registration directly.

Tell the provider about communication or accessibility preferences, such as written instructions, an authorised contact or advance notice. Ask whether they can accommodate the request and record agreed preferences in the service documentation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Brisbane directory listing mean a provider is available now?

No. Listings help people discover options, but capacity changes. Contact the organisation to confirm that it serves the required area and can offer the requested tasks and schedule.

### Can domestic assistance include personal care?

Not necessarily. Domestic assistance usually describes household help. Ask the provider whether personal care or clinical activities are offered, who would perform them and what qualifications or safeguards apply.

### How do I compare providers?

Compare the agreed tasks, worker continuity, availability, price, travel and cancellation terms, communication approach and complaint process. Ask each provider the same core questions and review the written service agreement before support begins.

### Is the dataset count a number of active SolDirectory providers?

No. The 685 figure above is the count shown in the supplied service-area register dataset for domestic assistance in Brisbane. It should not be read as the number of SolDirectory accounts, available providers or verified service outcomes.
